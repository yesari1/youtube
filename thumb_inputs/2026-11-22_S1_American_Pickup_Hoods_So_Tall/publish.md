Stand a four-year-old in front of a big American pickup and the hood can be taller than the child. Park the same child in front of a British hatchback and the bonnet stops at the waist. Why? In Britain, a new car's front has to be built to protect people on foot, so bonnets stay low and soft. America only proposed a rule like that in 2024, and its pickups kept growing. Some hoods now pass 40 inches, about the height of a four-year-old, and hoods that tall are about 45% more likely to kill a pedestrian they hit.
