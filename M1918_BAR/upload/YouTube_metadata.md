```text
In 1917 John Browning designed an automatic rifle to be fired while walking toward the enemy. It had only twenty rounds, weighed about twenty pounds by the Second World War, and its barrel could not be changed. Yet by 1944 every U.S. Marine rifle squad was built around three of them, and in Korea soldiers called it indispensable.

This is the story of the Browning Automatic Rifle: the French walking-fire idea behind it, how its gas system works (from a 1925 Army training film), how the Army turned it into a squad machine gun in 1938, how the Marines built the fire team around it, what it cost the men who carried it, and what S.L.A. Marshall found in Korea in 1951.
```
