# How Many Stuka Dive Bombers Does It Take to Sink One Modern Destroyer?

Planlandı: 2026-11-17 13:00 İstanbul (Zernio 6afc1c434d6883225a218e80, Warfront Archives)

## Açıklama
```
The Ju 87 Stuka was Germany's famous dive bomber of World War II. It dived almost straight down, its Jericho siren screaming, and dropped a 500 kg bomb. Off Crete in May 1941, air attacks led by dive bombers cost the Royal Navy six destroyers and three cruisers. But a modern Arleigh Burke-class destroyer is about five times heavier (about 9,200 tonnes against 1,700) and carries 96 vertical launch cells. Each Standard Missile can catch a Stuka more than 100 km away, so the first 96 Stukas never get close. Say three bomb hits sink her: Stukas 97, 98 and 99 have to hit. So it takes about a hundred Stukas, and that's only if every bomb hits.

Sources: RAF Museum and Encyclopaedia Britannica, Junkers Ju 87 (dive bomber, siren, 500 kg bomb); National Museum of the Royal Navy, Battle of Crete (six destroyers lost to air attack); US Navy fact files, Arleigh Burke-class destroyers (Flight IIA, 96 Mk 41 cells) and Standard Missile; CSIS Missile Threat (SM-2 range). The duel is a thought experiment: one missile per cell, one Stuka per missile, three hits to sink the ship and every bomb on target are assumptions. All scenes are explanatory drawings.
```
